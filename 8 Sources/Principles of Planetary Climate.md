**Principles of Planetary Climate - Chapter 1 Reference:** Pierrehumbert, R.T. (2010) Principles of Planetary Climate. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. 

> [!abstract] Abstract
> This chapter will survey a few of the major questions raised by observed features of present and past Earth and planetary climates. Some of these questions have been answered to one extent or another, but many remain largely unresolved. This will not be a comprehensive synopsis of Earth and planetary climate evolution; we will be content to point out a few striking facts about climate that demand a physical explanation. Then, in subsequent chapters, we'll develop the physics necessary to think about these problems. Although we hope not to be too Earth-centric in this book, in the present chapter we will perforce talk at greater length about Earth's climate than about those of other planets, because so much more is known about Earth's past climate than is known about the past climates of other planets. A careful study of Earth history suggests generalities that may apply to other planets, and also raises interesting questions about how things might have happened differently elsewhere, and it is with this goal in mind that we begin our journey.

## Close to Home

- explore why there is a $40^{\circ}\text{C}{}$ average temperature difference between north pole and equator
- we can compare temperatures to the moon to see difference atmosphere makes
	- moon doesn't have an atmosphere
	- without ocean or atmosphere the earth would experience much larger temperature swings
	- moon experiences higher highs and lower lows, but average temperature is lower
- if earth continuously absorbs solar energy it must also get rid of it would gradually heat up
- needed to find what components of atmosphere affects transmission of infrared radiation most
	- dominant components - nitrogen and oxygen are nearly transparent to infrared
	- *greenhouse gases* let solar energy through but stop radiation from leaving
		- minor components of atmosphere - CO2 and water vapor, methane
		- *long-lived* greenhouse gases removed slowly on scale of thousands of years
		- *short-lived* greenhouse gases removed on a time scale of weeks
