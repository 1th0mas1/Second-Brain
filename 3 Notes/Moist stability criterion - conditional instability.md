# Moist stability criterion - conditional instability

- expect atmosphere to be stable to convection if environemntal lapse rate $dT/dz{}$ has greater magnitude (more negative) than adiabatic lapse rate $(dT/dz)_{\text{ad}}{}$
	- lift parcel by 1km if atmosphere cools less than parcel then tends to sink - stable
- we derived two 'adiabatic' lapse rates - dry and moist pseudo-adiabat
- in reality stability of a parcel of gas depends on environmental lapse rate relative to both
![[Pasted image 20261004131521.png|476]]
1. $\Gamma{}<\Gamma{}_{\text{dry}}{}$: if lapse rate steeper (more negative) tha dry adiabat, the atmosphere is unstable to convection (ie adiabatic perturbations) regardless of relative to humidity
2. $\Gamma{}>\Gamma{}_{\text{moist}}{}$: if lapse rate is larger (less negative) than the moist adiabat, then atmosphere is absolute stable to convection regardless of relative to humididty
3. $\Gamma{}_{\text{dry}}<\Gamma{}<\Gamma{}_{\text{moist}}{}$: atmosphere is stable against dry convection but unstable to moist convection, implies unsaturated air parcels will return to original position if perturbed along dry adiabat
- lapse rate in troposphere lies between moist and dry adiabatic gradients
	- implies our troposphere is conditionally stable
	- moist ~$6$ K/km, dry ~$9.8$ K/km, observed global mean ~$8$ K/km
- stratospheres (by definition) are stable to convection
- Earth's atmosphere being conditionally unstable governed t[[Some moisture-related definitions to know]]hrough multiple processes
	- moist convection itself (leading to localised regions of rapid ascent)
	- mass conservation requiring descending (results relatively dry) that cools via radiation to space
![[Pasted image 20261005134046.png]]
- as background air radiates to space, the potential temperature $\theta{}{}$ decreasing during subsidence
	- leads to a conditionally unstable profile
- this causes convection on earth to be highly asymmetric with fast updrafts covering a small fraction of total area
	- ascent in narrow moist columns, descent over broad dry regions
		- eg hadley cell ascent in tropics, subtropical deserts under descent
			- hadley cell is only thermally direct cell, extends to $\pm30^{\circ}$
			- beyond this eddies dominate
		- regime set by Rossby number $\text{Ro}\equiv\frac{\text{advection}}{\text{rotation}}\sim\frac{U}{\Omega{}L}$
			- $\text{Ro}\gg1$ tropical, $\text{Ro}\ll1$ extratropical (rotation dominates)
			- $\text{Ro}\approx{}1$ at $\phi{}\sim{}30^{\circ}$ - edge of hadley cell
