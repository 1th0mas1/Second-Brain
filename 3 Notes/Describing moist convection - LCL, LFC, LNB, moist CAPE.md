# Describing moist convection - LCL, LFC, LNB, moist CAPE

- we show a typical temperature-altitude profile for a moist parcel (dashed line)
	- compare it to the dry adiabat (dotted line)
	- and a typical background (environmental) profile (solid line)
![[Pasted image 20261005140646.png]]
- point at which parcel first condenses and moves with dry adiabat (at lower altitudes) to the moist pseudo-adiabat (at higher altitudes)
	- lifting condensation level - LCL
	- where cloud deck can begin to form
	- corresponding rainout of moisture below the cloud deck
- saturation reached because ascent cools parcel which lowers $p_{\text{sat}}(T)$
	- not the pressure drop itself
	- cools quickly along dry adiabat, then more slowly once condensing
- saturation is two-way equilibrium between vapour and condensate
	- vapour still exists at saturation, can condense rather than must
	- simplistic view - ignores aerosols and supersaturation
- saturated parcels become buoyant (with respect to background environmental profile)
	- this is at level of free convection - LFC
	- saturated parcels undergo rapid ascent to the region where they dry out
		- parcel's temperature profile crosses the environmental profile once again
			- level of neutral buoyancy - LNB where parcel stops ascending
- note moist CAPE calculated in same way as dry CAPE ([[Convective (in)stability]])
	- key difference is parcel's temperature for moist CAPE with respect to moist rather than dry adiabat
	- other definitions are also now defined for moist instead of dry adiabat
