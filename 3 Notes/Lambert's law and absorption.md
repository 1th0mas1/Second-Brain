# Lambert's law and absorption

![[Pasted image 20261015121050.png|529]]
- consider radiation with initial radiance at given wavelength of $I_{\lambda{}}{}$ (or frequency $I_{\nu{}}{}$)
	- its absorbing/emitting slab with mass density $\rho{}{}$, absorption coefficient $k_{\nu{}}{}$ (units $\text{m}^{2}\text{kh}^{-1}{}$) and thermal radiance $B_{\nu{}}{}$ if emitting
- first consider purely absorbing $B_{\nu{}}=0{}$
	- Lambert's law is empirical relationship showing fractional change in radiance due to absorption is proportional to the mass per area of slab
$$
\text{Lambert's law: }dI_{\nu{}}=-I_{\nu{}}k_{\nu{}}\rho{}dl
$$
- define *absorptivity* at a given frequency
	- fraction of incident radiance of a given frequency absorbed by material
	- for case of infinitesimal slab we see $a_{\nu{}}=k_{\nu{}}\rho{}dl{}$
$$
dI_{\nu{}}=-I_{\nu{}}a_{\nu{}}
$$
