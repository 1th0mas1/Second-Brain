# Quantum Harmonic Oscillator and the Hydrogen Atom

## Ladder Operator Algebra

Near any potential minimum, a Maclaurin expansion gives $V(x)\approx V_0+\tfrac12 kx^2$, so $\hat H=\hat p^2/2m+\tfrac12 k\hat x^2$ — the harmonic oscillator is the universal small-oscillation Hamiltonian. Rather than solving the differential equation directly (which gives Hermite-polynomial solutions $\psi_n(x)\propto H_n(x)e^{-x^2/4l^2}$), define
$$a=\frac{m\omega\hat x+i\hat p}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}},\qquad a^\dagger=\frac{m\omega\hat x-i\hat p}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}$$
Using $[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$ gives $[a,a^\dagger]=1$, and direct substitution shows $a^\dagger a=\hat H/\hbar\omega-\tfrac12$, so
$$\hat H = \hbar\omega\left(a^\dagger a+\tfrac12\right)=\hbar\omega\left(\hat N+\tfrac12\right)$$
where $\hat N=a^\dagger a$ is the Hermitian **number operator** ($a$ itself is not Hermitian). From $[\hat N,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger$, $[\hat N,a]=-a$ it follows that $[\hat H,a^\dagger]=\hbar\omega a^\dagger$, so if $\hat H|E\rangle=E|E\rangle$ then $\hat H(a^\dagger|E\rangle)=(E+\hbar\omega)(a^\dagger|E\rangle)$: $a^\dagger$ (**creation**) raises the energy by exactly $\hbar\omega$, and similarly $a$ (**annihilation**) lowers it by $\hbar\omega$.

## Spectrum and Ground State

The lowering tower cannot continue indefinitely — since $\langle\hat H\rangle=\hbar\omega(\langle\hat N\rangle+\tfrac12)$ must stay non-negative, there must be a ground state $|0\rangle$ annihilated by $a$: $a|0\rangle=0$. Solving this first-order equation gives the Gaussian ground-state wavefunction, with energy $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$ (the irreducible **zero-point energy** — a purely quantum effect with no classical analogue, a direct consequence of the uncertainty principle). The full spectrum is then generated by repeated raising: $E_n=(n+\tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ and $|n\rangle=\frac{(a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|0\rangle$, with excited-state wavefunctions $\psi_n(x)\propto H_n(x/\sqrt2\,l)\,\psi_0(x)$ — the Gaussian acting as an envelope. This ladder-operator algebra is *exactly* what's reused to quantise a free scalar field (each field mode becomes an independent harmonic oscillator; $a^\dagger,a$ become particle creation/annihilation operators) — the direct bridge from single-particle QM to QFT.

## Separation of Variables

The two-body problem (electron + nucleus, Coulomb potential $V=-Ze^2/4\pi\epsilon_0 r$) reduces via centre-of-mass coordinates and reduced mass $\mu=m_em_N/(m_e+m_N)$ to a free-particle piece plus a relative-coordinate piece $H(\vec r)=\hat p_r^2/2\mu - Ze^2/4\pi\epsilon_0 r$, which is spherically symmetric: $[\hat L^2,H]=[\hat L_z,H]=0$. Since angular momentum operators generate rotations, any rotationally-invariant Hamiltonian commutes with them — the general template for *any* central potential. This lets energy eigenstates be labelled $|n,l,m\rangle$ and the wavefunction separated $\psi=R_{nl}(r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)$, reducing the 3D Schrödinger equation to a 1D radial equation with an effective centrifugal barrier $\hbar^2l(l+1)/2\mu r^2$.

## Radial Equation and Energy Levels

Rescaling $\rho=r/a_Z$ (with $a_Z=4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2/\mu Ze^2$ the Bohr-like radius), the large-$\rho$ behaviour is $R_{nl}\sim e^{-\lambda_{nl}\rho}$ and the small-$\rho$ behaviour is $R_{nl}\sim\rho^l$ (the other indicial root, $\rho^{-l-1}$, isn't normalisable). The full ansatz $R_{nl}(\rho)=\rho^l e^{-\lambda_{nl}\rho}F_{nl}(\rho)$ turns the equation into the generalised Laguerre equation, whose polynomial solutions exist only when $1/\lambda_{nl}-l-1$ is a non-negative integer — this quantisation condition forces $\lambda_{nl}=1/n$ for integer $n>l\ge0$. The result is quantised energy levels depending *only* on the principal quantum number $n$:
$$E_n = -\frac{\mu}{m_e}Z^2\frac{E_R}{n^2},\qquad E_R = \frac{m_ee^4}{32\pi^2\epsilon_0^2\hbar^2}=13.6\,\text{eV}$$
with $l=0,...,n-1$ and $m=-l,...,l$. This $l$-independence (an "accidental" degeneracy specific to the pure $1/r$ potential, lifted by relativistic and other corrections) gives level $n$ a total degeneracy $\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(2l+1)=n^2$. Photon emission/absorption between levels follows $E_\gamma=E_a-E_b\propto(1/b^2-1/a^2)$, the empirical Rydberg formula. The separable-spherical-potential template (radial equation + spherical harmonics for the angular part) generalises to any central potential, not just Coulomb.
