# Kirchhoff's law and emission

![[Pasted image 20261015153653.png]]
- consider again *emissivity* $\epsilon{}_{\nu{}}{}$
	- ratio of radiance emitted by a substance to blackbody radiance $B_{\nu{}}{}$
	- blackbodies have $\epsilon{}_{\nu{}}=1{}$
	- real substances  have $\epsilon{}_{\nu{}}<1{}$
- Kirchhoff's law states:
	- substances in thermodynamic equilibrium emit as efficiently as they absorb
$$
\text{Kirchoff's law: } \epsilon{}_{\nu{}}=a_{\nu{}}
$$
- Kirchhoff's law is only valid is the substance is in **thermodynamic equilibrium**
	- consequence of the *second law of thermodynamics*
		- object with $\epsilon{}\ne a{}$ placed in parcel of gas molecules then conduction attempts to equalise the temperatures while radiation will try to make them different
- emission from an infinitesimal slab is $dI_{\nu{}}=\epsilon{}_{\nu{}}B_{\nu{}}{}$
	- Kirchhoff law means $dI_{\nu{}}=a_{\nu{}}B_{\nu{}}{}$ thus change in emitted radiation is
$$
\text{emitting - only slab: }dI_{\nu{}}=B_{\nu{}}k_{\nu{}}\rho{}dl
$$
